Bridging the Semantic Gap: Evidential Fusion in Multimodal NLP

Muhammad Hamza, Krishna Rai, Sobhan Sarkar
IIT Kharagpur & IIM Ranchi

A deep dive into how we taught neural networks to "watch" movies alongside human reviewers, dynamically aligning text with visual frames, and mathematically resolving subjective ambiguity using Dempster-Shafer theory.

1. The Blindness of Text Models

Imagine reading a review for Christopher Nolan's Interstellar. The reviewer writes: "The sheer isolation of humanity was perfectly captured in that single, silent wide shot."

If you feed this review into a standard Natural Language Processing (NLP) model, it registers the positive sentiment but fundamentally misses the context. [cite_start]This is what we define as the semantic gap in high-context domains like film critique[cite: 28]. [cite_start]Reviewers consistently reference complex visual elements—such as lighting, camera angles, or symbolic framing—that are entirely absent from the text itself[cite: 28, 71].

[cite_start]

Predicting review helpfulness is a critical challenge impacting both NLP and Recommender Systems (RS)[cite: 27]. [cite_start]In traditional Recommender Systems, platforms rely heavily on crowd-sourced "helpful" votes[cite: 23]. [cite_start]However, relying solely on user-voted helpfulness introduces severe noise and bias, frequently overlooking high-quality reviews simply due to low visibility or limited early feedback[cite: 23, 29]. [cite_start]Early multimodal frameworks, such as Multi-perspective Coherent Reasoning (MCR), attempted to integrate visual cues but operated on a flawed assumption: they assumed that a review's paired image is inherently aligned with its text[cite: 56, 70]. [cite_start]To build a robust multimodal solution, we realized we must overcome two foundational obstacles: explicit Visual-Textual Semantic Grounding (dynamically linking abstract review text to actual movie frames) and Uncertainty Management (handling the inherent noise of user-generated content and conflicting modality signals)[cite: 30].

2. Visual Grounding via CLIP

[cite_start]

To eliminate the assumption of inherent multimodal correspondence, we proposed a semantic alignment module utilizing the joint vision-language space of Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)[cite: 14, 78]. [cite_start]We extracted approximately 24,000 keyframes across our dataset and projected both the user's text and candidate movie stills into a shared latent dimensional space[cite: 106]. Instead of arbitrary pairings, the model actively searches for the frame that best grounds the text's abstract references.

[cite_start]

The semantic alignment score between a review $r_i$ and a candidate frame $f_j$ is computed using the cosine similarity between their respective CLIP embeddings[cite: 143]. We select the most relevant frame $f^*$ by maximizing this similarity function:

$$ align(r_i, f_j) = \cos(CLIP_{text}(r_i), CLIP_{image}(f_j)) $$
$$ f^* = \arg\max_{f} \cos(e_r, e_f) $$
[cite_start]

To address potential bias introduced by CLIP-based pseudo-labeling, we manually validated a subset of 500 review-image pairs and optimized the similarity threshold[cite: 145]. [cite_start]We empirically determined a helpfulness threshold of $0.54$, derived from IMDb vote ratios[cite: 107, 117]. [cite_start]This precise threshold balances class distribution while ensuring semantic relevance, as higher thresholds severely unbalanced the classes and reduced dataset size[cite: 118, 120]. Here is how this dynamic frame selection operates in PyTorch:

import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def get_optimal_frame(review_emb, frame_embs):
    """
    review_emb: Tensor (1, 512) from CLIP text encoder
    frame_embs: Tensor (N, 512) from CLIP image encoder (N keyframes)
    """
    # Normalize embeddings for cosine similarity computation
    review_emb = F.normalize(review_emb, p=2, dim=-1)
    frame_embs = F.normalize(frame_embs, p=2, dim=-1)
    
    # Compute similarity scores across all candidate frames
    similarity_scores = torch.matmul(review_emb, frame_embs.T)
    
    # Select the frame f* that maximizes semantic alignment
    best_frame_idx = torch.argmax(similarity_scores, dim=-1)
    alignment_score = similarity_scores[0, best_frame_idx]
    
    return best_frame_idx, alignment_score

3. Architectural Feature Extraction

[cite_start]

With the optimally aligned frame and text acquired, we process a curated dataset of 40,000 IMDb user reviews, meticulously cleaned to retain deep semantic content[cite: 105]. [cite_start]Our framework integrates advanced deep learning techniques, leveraging distinct state-of-the-art transformer encoders for independent modality extraction before fusion[cite: 88, 100].

[cite_start]

The textual analysis component utilizes Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) for profound contextual review encoding[cite: 14, 121]. [cite_start]For each review $r_i$, the BERT encoder maps the sequence into a $d_{bert}$-dimensional real-valued vector space, resulting in $h_{text} \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$[cite: 125, 127, 130]. [cite_start]Simultaneously, the visual analysis employs a Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture to extract high-level semantic representations from the chosen movie scene, producing $h_{visual} \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$ for the selected frame $f^*$[cite: 131, 133, 135].

Fig 1: The complete multimodal pipeline. Text reviews are encoded using BERT, while keyframes are processed via ViT, guided by CLIP alignment.

[cite_start]

The network utilizes two task-specific heads: a binary or regression output for helpfulness prediction and a binary classifier for semantic alignment[cite: 179, 180]. [cite_start]Both heads share the fused representation and are optimized jointly using a multi-task loss formulation: $\mathcal{L} = \lambda_1 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{help} + \lambda_2 \cdot \mathcal{L}_{align}$[cite: 181, 182]. [cite_start]We optimize the network using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 3e-5 and a batch size of 16, leveraging its adaptive update mechanism for stable convergence[cite: 185].

4. Mathematical Rigor: Evidential Fusion

[cite_start]

Modern multimodal review models frequently employ deterministic fusion strategies, such as simple concatenation, element-wise addition, or fixed attention mechanisms[cite: 73]. [cite_start]These shallow deterministic methods possess a critical flaw: they overlook modality-level uncertainty[cite: 74]. [cite_start]In user-generated content, reviewers are frequently hyperbolic, and visuals may be abstract or ambiguous[cite: 74]. [cite_start]Deterministic models risk overfitting to one modality, forcing overconfident predictions even when inputs aggressively contradict one another[cite: 75].

[cite_start]

To move beyond these limitations, we engineered an uncertainty-aware fusion framework grounded in Dempster-Shafer theory[cite: 80, 174]. [cite_start]This belief-theoretic strategy interprets the extracted textual and visual embeddings as distinct "opinions" or belief masses[cite: 159, 172]. [cite_start]When the textual and visual cues agree, the belief mass is structurally reinforced; however, when the modalities conflict, the framework explicitly quantifies this disagreement as conflict mass $K$[cite: 166, 173].

$$ K = \sum_{i \neq j} m_1(i) \cdot m_2(j) $$
$$ m_{fused} = \frac{m_1 \cdot m_2}{1 - K} $$
[cite_start]

In these equations, $K$ quantifies the total conflict by summing over all distinct hypothesis pairs where $i \neq j$[cite: 167, 171]. [cite_start]The fused belief mass $m_{fused}$ is then computed using orthogonal combination rules[cite: 160, 164]. [cite_start]By dividing by $(1 - K)$, the architecture adaptively weights the modalities based on confidence, yielding exceptionally robust predictions under noisy or ambiguous conditions[cite: 81, 173].

class EvidentialFusionLayer(torch.nn.Module):
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__()
        
    def forward(self, mass_text, mass_visual):
        # Calculate agreement (element-wise multiplication of beliefs)
        joint_mass = mass_text * mass_visual 
        
        # Calculate conflict K: sum of non-matching hypothesis multiplications
        K = (mass_text[:, 0] * mass_visual[:, 1]) + (mass_text[:, 1] * mass_visual[:, 0])
        K = K.unsqueeze(-1) 
        
        # Dempster's Rule of Combination: Normalize by (1 - K)
        # 1e-8 added for numerical stability
        m_fused = joint_mass / (1 - K + 1e-8)
        
        return m_fused

5. Empirical Results & Ablation

[cite_start]

To rigorously evaluate the framework, we conducted a comprehensive set of experiments across nine diverse movies, encompassing genres such as sci-fi (Interstellar), drama (Shawshank Redemption), and crime (The Godfather)[cite: 191, 195]. [cite_start]Evidential fusion achieved state-of-the-art ranking performance with a Mean Average Precision (MAP) of $0.87$ and a Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG) of $0.97$[cite: 16].

Crucially, model behavior varied significantly based on narrative style. [cite_start]In visually dense and abstract films like Interstellar and Fellowship of the Ring, Evidential Fusion vastly outperformed baselines (e.g., MAP: $0.90$, F1: $0.80$ for Interstellar) because of its mathematical strength in managing modality uncertainty and aligning abstract text with symbolic visuals[cite: 204, 205]. [cite_start]Conversely, for text-heavy, dialogue-driven narratives like The Godfather, attention-based fusion yielded the highest accuracy[cite: 200, 210]. [cite_start]Action-centric films with clear visual-text alignments, like The Dark Knight, responded well to simple concatenation[cite: 212].

We executed rigorous ablation studies to isolate component impacts. [cite_start]Removing CLIP-based semantic alignment drastically reduced the F1-score by 4%[cite: 228, 229]. To test robustness, we conducted leave-one-out validation where 20% of the visual data was deliberately dropped. [cite_start]Under this corruption, evidential fusion's performance declined by a mere 2.7%, whereas baseline multimodal methods suffered a 5.2% decay[cite: 225, 226]. [cite_start]Finally, paired t-tests aggregated across all 9 movies proved that Evidential Fusion's improvements are consistently statistically significant ($p < 0.05$) compared to attention fusion across F1, MAP, and NDCG metrics[cite: 230, 231, 243].
